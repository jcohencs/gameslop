# Feature Specification: Graphics Overhaul

**Feature Branch**: `claude/determined-gates-4azszv`

**Created**: 2026-10-03

**Status**: Draft

**Design pillars**: Silly (a readable cartoon look), plus support for Hustle, Escape and Brawl through clearer visuals

**Input**: User description: "Graphics overhaul: a large visual upgrade, all still generated in code (no imported assets). (1) Stylized cartoon rendering: toon/cel-shaded materials with rim light on everything, ink outlines on characters and key props, per-location lighting moods (sun angle/color, sky, fog, glow, color grading); (2) procedural textures (grass, asphalt, floor tiles, wood, brick, carpet, concrete) generated in code and applied to level surfaces; (3) character art pass: real eyes (whites + pupils), mouths, noses, cheeks, hair styles, clothing details (collars, belts, sleeves, shoe soles), and expressions (angry brows when adults are alerted, sad/crying faces); (4) environment dressing: wind-swaying grass, fuller trees and bushes, clouds, lamp posts, props per location; (5) VFX: hit spark particles, cartoon dizzy stars over stunned/KO'd adults, tear drops on crying kids, sparkles around the whale, ambient dust motes indoors, shimmering extract beams; (6) a graphics quality setting (High/Low) that scales outlines, grass density, shadows and particles so it runs on weaker machines."

## User Scenarios & Testing *(mandatory)*

### User Story 1 - A Cohesive Cartoon Look (Priority: P1)

Every surface and character is drawn in one stylized cartoon style: banded (cel) lighting, a soft
rim highlight on edges, and dark ink outlines around characters and important props. Each location
has its own lighting mood:

- **Playground:** sunny midday.
- **Locals:** warm evening.
- **Pizza Party Palace:** colorful party lights.
- **Mall:** bright, cool daylight.

**Why this priority**: the shared style is the foundation every other visual change builds on, and it
gives the biggest jump in quality.

**Independent Test**: deploy to each location and confirm that characters have outlines and banded
shading, and that the mood (sun color/angle, sky, fog, color grading) differs between locations.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** any raid, **When** the player looks at a kid or adult, **Then** the character has a
   dark outline and cel-banded shading.
2. **Given** two different locations, **When** each raid starts, **Then** their sun color/angle,
   sky colors and fog/grading differ noticeably.
3. **Given** the hideout or title screen, **When** shown, **Then** the UI is unchanged and readable.

---

### User Story 2 - Textured Surfaces (Priority: P1)

Ground and walls stop being flat colors. The ground and walls show patterns that fit each place:

- grass with blade noise and asphalt with speckle;
- checker floor tiles with grout and wood with grain;
- brick walls, patterned carpet and concrete.

The patterns are generated by the game itself and tile cleanly at a sensible scale.

**Why this priority**: flat untextured surfaces are the most "unfinished" part of the current look.

**Independent Test**: check each location for at least two distinct textures and confirm they tile
without obvious stretching.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** the Playground, **When** viewed, **Then** the grass and the blacktop court show
   textures.
2. **Given** the Locals, **When** viewed, **Then** the parking lot shows asphalt, the store floor
   shows a floor texture, and the walls show brick.
3. **Given** a textured wall of any size, **When** viewed up close, **Then** the pattern keeps a
   consistent scale (no stretching on long walls).

---

### User Story 3 - Characters with Personality (Priority: P2)

Kids and adults get real faces and clothing details:

- **Faces:** white eyes with pupils, a nose, a mouth, and rosy cheeks on kids.
- **Hair:** several hair styles.
- **Clothes:** collars, belts, sleeves and shoe soles.

Faces react: adults' brows tilt angry and their mouths frown when they're chasing or attacking, and
crying kids look sad.

**Why this priority**: characters are what the player looks at most. Expressions make the AI's
state readable at a glance (Constitution VIII).

**Independent Test**: look at a calm adult, then an alerted one, and see the expression change. Look
at a crying kid and see a sad face.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** any character, **When** viewed up close, **Then** eyes (whites + pupils), nose and
   mouth are visible.
2. **Given** an adult switches from patrolling to chasing, **When** viewed, **Then** their brows
   tilt angry and their mouth frowns.
3. **Given** a kid is crying, **When** viewed, **Then** they have a sad face and visible tear drops.
4. **Given** a crowd of kids, **When** viewed, **Then** at least three hair styles appear.

---

### User Story 4 - Living Environments (Priority: P2)

Locations feel populated and alive:

- grass sways in the wind;
- trees have fuller canopies, and bushes appear;
- clouds drift in the sky;
- lamp posts glow;
- each location gets themed props (trash cans and benches; posters and chairs; balloons and
  streamers).

**Why this priority**: it makes places feel like places (Constitution VI) instead of block-outs.

**Independent Test**: in each location, confirm the extra dressing exists and the grass and clouds
move over time.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** the Playground, **When** watched for a few seconds, **Then** the grass visibly sways and
   clouds move.
2. **Given** each location, **When** viewed, **Then** it contains themed props that weren't there
   before.
3. **Given** the dressing props, **When** adults chase the player, **Then** pathing still works
   (decor that blocks movement is part of the navigable layout).

---

### User Story 5 - Juicy Effects (Priority: P3)

Visual effects make every action feel good:

- spark bursts on hits;
- cartoon dizzy stars circling over stunned and knocked-out adults;
- tear drops falling from crying kids;
- gold sparkles around the whale;
- floating dust motes in indoor locations;
- extract beams that shimmer and pulse.

**Why this priority**: it extends the "feel before features" principle to the visuals.

**Independent Test**: trigger each event and see its effect.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** the player lands a hit, **When** it connects, **Then** a spark burst appears at the
   impact.
2. **Given** an adult is stunned or knocked out, **When** viewed, **Then** stars circle their head
   until they recover or disappear.
3. **Given** the whale kid, **When** viewed, **Then** gold sparkles float around them.
4. **Given** an indoor location, **When** viewed, **Then** dust motes drift in the air.
5. **Given** an active extract, **When** viewed, **Then** its beam visibly pulses.

---

### User Story 6 - Graphics Quality Setting (Priority: P2)

Settings has a Graphics Quality option (High / Low). Low turns off outlines, cuts grass density by
at least 75%, disables shadows, and reduces particles, so the game stays playable on weak machines.
The choice persists.

**Why this priority**: the upgrade adds rendering cost, and players need a way to scale it back.

**Independent Test**: switch to Low and confirm outlines, shadows and most grass are gone. Switch to
High and confirm they return. Restart and confirm the choice persists.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** quality is High, **When** a raid starts, **Then** outlines, shadows, full grass and
   all particles are on.
2. **Given** quality is Low, **When** a raid starts, **Then** outlines and shadows are off, grass is
   at most 25% of the High count, and particle counts are reduced.
3. **Given** the player changes quality and restarts, **When** the game loads, **Then** the chosen
   quality is still selected.

### Edge Cases

- **Changing quality mid-raid:** it applies from the next raid. The settings panel says so.
- **Compatibility renderer:** effects that renderer can't draw (e.g. screen-space AO) are skipped
  without errors, and the toon look still applies.
- **Characters flashing white when hit:** still readable with outlines and toon shading.
- **Outlines on the first-person arms:** off, to avoid a thick black border filling the screen.
- **Effects during pause:** they freeze along with the level.
- **Very long walls or floors:** textures repeat at world scale rather than stretching.

## Requirements *(mandatory)*

### Functional Requirements

**Rendering style**

- **FR-001**: All world and character materials MUST use banded (cel) lighting with a rim highlight.
- **FR-002**: Characters (kids, adults) and key props (extract beams excluded) MUST show a dark ink
  outline when quality is High.
- **FR-003**: Each location MUST define a lighting mood (sun direction, sun color and energy, sky
  top/horizon colors, ambient level, fog, contrast/saturation grading, glow), applied at raid start.
  The hideout and title use a default mood.

**Textures**

- **FR-004**: The game MUST generate at least seven surface textures in code: grass, asphalt, floor
  tiles, wood, brick, carpet, concrete.
- **FR-005**: Textures MUST tile at a consistent world scale regardless of surface size.
- **FR-006**: Each location MUST use at least two distinct textures on its large surfaces.

**Characters**

- **FR-007**: Characters MUST have eye whites with pupils, a nose and a mouth. Kids MUST have
  cheeks.
- **FR-008**: Characters MUST support at least four hair styles (e.g. none/bald, bowl, spiky,
  ponytail/bun), plus caps and hats.
- **FR-009**: Clothing MUST include a collar, a belt and shoe soles.
- **FR-010**: Expressions MUST include neutral, angry (alerted/attacking adults) and sad (crying or
  scammed kids), switching automatically with state.

**Environment**

- **FR-011**: Grass areas MUST have swaying grass tufts. Density follows quality.
- **FR-012**: Every location MUST include drifting clouds in the sky, plus themed props: lamp posts
  and bushes outdoors; posters, chairs and balloons where they fit the theme.
- **FR-013**: Props that block movement MUST be solid and included in AI pathing. Purely decorative
  props MUST NOT block movement.

**Effects**

- **FR-014**: Effects MUST include hit sparks, dizzy stars (stunned or KO'd adults), tears (crying
  kids), whale sparkles, indoor dust motes and pulsing extract beams.
- **FR-015**: Particle amounts MUST scale down at Low quality.

**Quality setting**

- **FR-016**: Settings MUST offer Graphics Quality: High / Low, saved with the other settings.
- **FR-017**: Low quality MUST disable outlines and shadows, cut grass to at most 25% of High, and
  reduce particles by at least half.

**Constraints**

- **FR-018**: All visuals MUST be generated in code (Constitution X). No imported image, model or
  font files.
- **FR-019**: Gameplay, AI behavior and all existing smoke tests MUST stay unchanged and passing.

### Key Entities

- **Visual mood**: per-location lighting and grading settings.
- **Procedural texture**: a named, generated pattern with a tiling scale.
- **Character look**: hair style, clothing details and current expression.
- **Quality profile**: High or Low, with outline, shadow, grass and particle scaling values.

## Success Criteria *(mandatory)*

### Measurable Outcomes

- **SC-001**: 100% of characters in a raid show outlines (High) and banded shading. 0% show outlines
  on Low.
- **SC-002**: All 4 locations have distinct moods: no two share the same sun color and sky colors.
- **SC-003**: At least 7 distinct generated textures exist, and each location uses at least 2.
- **SC-004**: An alerted adult's expression differs from a patrolling one within 0.5 s of the state
  change.
- **SC-005**: Low quality has at most 25% of High's grass instances, no shadows, no outlines, and at
  most 50% of High's particle counts.
- **SC-006**: Every listed effect appears when its trigger happens (6 effect types).
- **SC-007**: All previously passing smoke checks still pass.

## Assumptions

- The style is "bright cartoon" (in the spirit of Fortnite or Fall Guys), not realistic.
- Indoor locations stay open to the sky (no ceilings), which keeps lighting simple and the camera
  clear. Mood lighting and practical lights sell the interior feel.
- The first-person arms keep toon shading but no outlines.
- Low quality targets integrated graphics. High targets a mid-range GPU at 60 fps.
- Quality changes apply when the next raid starts.
